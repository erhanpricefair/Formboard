# 1. Legal, Consent & Safety

> **Important:** These are practical, industry-standard templates written to be clear and fair. They are not legal advice. Before you run your first paid class, have the consent form and your terms reviewed once by a local solicitor or your insurer — most insurers will review wording for free and it typically costs under an hour of a solicitor's time. Check your local requirements for working with minors (in Australia, a Working With Children Check / Blue Card is commonly required or expected when running classes for under-18s — get one even if it is not strictly mandatory; parents look for it).

---

## 1.1 Non-negotiable rules (print these and keep them in your kit)

- No student under 16 attends without a completed consent form signed by a parent or guardian.
- Under-16s: a parent/guardian either stays on site, or provides a contactable phone number and a named pickup person.
- No treatments that break the skin, ever. No lash extensions, no waxing, no tinting, no piercing, no needling, no chemical peels. You teach cosmetic application only.
- No product goes from a container to a face twice. Decant, use a spatula, use disposables.
- Nobody applies makeup to another person's face in a beginner class. Everyone works on themselves, in their own mirror. This removes the single largest source of hygiene and consent complaints.
- You do not touch a student's face without asking out loud and getting a verbal yes, every single time — even if they said yes ten minutes earlier.
- No photograph is taken, kept or posted without the photo release box being ticked. A verbal "sure" on the day is not enough for a minor.

## 1.4 Hygiene protocol — the version you actually follow

### Before every class (30 minutes)
- [ ] Wipe every table surface, mirror, chair arm and door handle with a disinfectant wipe.
- [ ] Set out a labelled station per student: mirror, headband, disposable applicators, cotton pads, tissue, small mirror, hand sanitiser, waste bag.
- [ ] Decant all cream and liquid products into individual palettes/tiles with a clean spatula — one tile per student, never returned to the source container.
- [ ] Sharpen every pencil to a fresh surface immediately before use.
- [ ] Sanitise powder products by wiping the pan surface with 70% isopropyl alcohol and letting it dry.
- [ ] Brushes: one clean set per student, freshly washed and fully dried. Never a shared set.
- [ ] Check expiry dates. Mascara ≤3 months open, liquid liner ≤3 months, foundation ≤12 months, powders ≤24 months. Bin anything borderline.
- [ ] Confirm the room has a hand-washing sink or, failing that, two pump bottles of sanitiser and a bin with a lid.

### The rules you teach out loud during the class
These are content, not admin — students value them and they are what makes you look like a professional rather than a hobbyist.

1. **Never double-dip.** Product comes out once, onto a palette or a disposable. Anything that has touched a face does not go back.
2. **Mascara wands are single-use.** One wand, one eye job, then the bin. Never back into the tube.
3. **Lipstick is decanted or applied with a disposable brush** when anyone other than the owner is involved.
4. **Pencils get sharpened between people**, always.
5. **Sponges are personal and washable.** A damp sponge is a bacteria farm — dry it fully, wash it after every use, replace it monthly.
6. **Brushes: spot-clean daily, deep-clean weekly.** Face brushes weekly at minimum, eye brushes more often.
7. **Never use makeup on broken, infected, weeping or sunburnt skin**, and never over an active cold sore — a cold sore contaminates the whole kit.
8. **Eyes:** if it stings, it comes off. Immediately, with water and a clean cotton pad. Do not push through discomfort.
9. **Hands get sanitised** before you start, after touching your phone, and between sections.
10. **Hair goes back** before skin prep — headbands are provided.

### After every class
- [ ] Bag and remove all waste, including disposables.
- [ ] Wash all brushes with brush shampoo, reshape, dry bristles-down or flat overnight.
- [ ] Disinfect palettes, spatulas, tiles, tweezers, sharpeners.
- [ ] Wipe down tables, mirrors, chairs.
- [ ] Launder all towels, headbands and capes at 60°C.
- [ ] Restock disposables so your kit is class-ready before you leave.
- [ ] Write anything notable in your incident/observation log (see 1.6).

## 1.5 Professional boundaries — especially with teenagers

You are, for three hours, an adult with authority over a room that may contain 14-year-olds. Behave accordingly and document it, because it protects both the students and you.

**Physical boundaries**
- Teach from the front and from your own face. Demonstrate on yourself in a mirror, or use a large screen/phone-on-tripod so people can see close work.
- Ask before every touch, out loud, using a full sentence: *"Would it be okay if I blended this along your jaw for you?"* Wait for a spoken yes.
- Touch only the face, hands, and forearms. Never the neck, shoulders, back, hair beyond a headband adjustment, or anywhere else.
- If a student flinches, hesitates, or says "um" — stop, smile, and hand them the brush instead. No comment, no fuss.

**Verbal boundaries**
- Never comment on a student's weight, body, acne, scarring, features being "too" anything, or how much they "need" makeup.
- Reframe every correction as a technique note about the product, not the person. Not *"your eyes are very hooded, that won't work"* — instead *"on a hooded eye shape, we place this a few millimetres higher so it stays visible when the eye is open. Here's how to find your placement line."*
- Never diagnose skin. You are not a dermatologist. The phrase is: *"That's outside what I can advise on — a pharmacist or a GP is the right person for that, and they see it constantly."*
- Do not discuss cosmetic injectables, weight, dieting, or "fixing" features with under-18s. If a teen raises it, redirect warmly: *"Honestly, at your age the highest-value skill is skin care and light-handed application. That's what we're doing today."*

**Digital boundaries**
- One business account. Never a personal account, never a personal number.
- Do not follow, DM, or accept DMs from under-18 students on personal channels. All contact with a minor goes through the parent's phone or email.
- Never post a minor's face without the ticked release, and never post a minor's face alongside their handle plus your venue location in the same post.
- Do not offer under-18s private one-to-one sessions in a private residence. Private lessons for minors happen in the community space, with a parent present or on site.

**Safeguarding**
- Keep the room visible. Doors open or windows uncovered where practical. Avoid ever being alone with a single minor in a closed room.
- If a student discloses something concerning (self-harm, abuse, an eating disorder), do not investigate and do not promise secrecy. Listen, thank them, and say: *"Thank you for telling me. I'm not the right person to help with this properly, but I want to make sure you get someone who is."* Then follow your local mandatory reporting pathway and inform the parent unless the parent is the concern, in which case contact the relevant child protection line.
- Log the incident the same day, factually, in your incident log.

## 1.7 Running mixed-age and age-banded classes

**Default recommendation: age-band your classes.** It is easier to teach, easier to market, and eliminates most behavioural friction. Run three clearly named products:

| Class | Ages | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fresh Faces** | 13–17 | 3 hrs (or 2.5 with a longer break) | Skin prep heavy, minimal-coverage base, soft brown eye, tinted lip. No heavy contour, no strip lash, no full-glam. Parent consent compulsory. |
| **The Everyday Masterclass** | 18+ | 3 hrs | Your flagship. Full curriculum as written, including the evening variation. |
| **Mother & Daughter** | 12+ with an adult | 3 hrs | Sold as a pair. Highest-margin, highest-emotion, best word-of-mouth product you have. |

**If you do run genuinely mixed ages (adults and teens together):**

- Cap the class at 8 and require at least 2 adults if any minor is present.
- Seat teens together, not scattered — they participate more and feel less watched.
- Set the tone in the first two minutes: *"We've got a mix of ages in the room today, which is my favourite kind of class. The rule is simple — we're all beginners here, nobody comments on anyone else's face, and everybody gets to ask the 'silly' question."*
- Pitch the teaching to the youngest person in the room and let the adults enjoy the clarity. Adults never complain that something was explained too simply; teens absolutely disengage when it is explained too fast.
- Modify content live: when you reach the evening variation, offer the teens a "school formal / soft glam" version — same techniques, less intensity — rather than excluding them.
- Never single a teenager out as an example, positively or negatively. Adults handle being a demo model; teens can find it excruciating.
- Watch the pace of the room, not the clock. Teens finish faster and get restless; adults are slower and ask more questions. Give the fast finishers a "now try it with your other hand / now try it in 90 seconds" challenge rather than letting the room drift.
- Refreshments: nut-free by default, and check the dietary line on the form.

**Ratios and staffing**
- 1 instructor to 8 students is the comfortable maximum for a hands-on beginner class.
- Above 8, bring a paid assistant. Above 10, the class stops being a masterclass and becomes a demonstration — price and describe it differently.
- If any student is under 16 and no parent is staying, have a second adult present in the room. This is basic safeguarding practice and costs you very little.
