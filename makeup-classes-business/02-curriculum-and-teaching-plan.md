# 2. Curriculum & Teaching Plan
## The Everyday Masterclass — 3 hours, complete beginner to confident

---

## 2.1 Design logic (why the plan is built this way)

- **One skill, one demo, one practice round, one check.** Never demo two things before letting them try. Beginners lose the first thing the moment they hear the second.
- **Halves, not wholes.** Students work on one half of the face while you talk, then match the other half themselves. They see the difference instantly, which teaches more than any explanation.
- **Adults' attention holds for about 20 minutes.** Every block is 15–25 minutes with a change of state — you talk, they do, they look up.
- **The evening variation is deliberately last and deliberately fast.** It is the "wow" that makes people post about the class, and it works because it is built on the base they already made.
- **Removal is taught, not mentioned.** It is the single most skipped topic in beauty education and the one that most affects their skin over the next year. It also sends them home with a calm, clean face, which prevents the "I looked overdone walking to my car" feeling.

**Room setup:** semicircle or long table facing you. Each student: desk mirror, headband, brush set, palette tile, disposables, cotton pads, water. You: your own station front and centre, plus a phone on a tripod projecting to a TV/laptop screen if the venue allows — close eye work is invisible from three metres away.

## 2.2 The minute-by-minute plan

> Times are elapsed minutes from the start. Bold headings are the blocks; the indented lines are your actual teaching beats.

---

### BLOCK 0 — Arrival and settle · 0:00–0:10

**0:00–0:05 — Doors, greeting, seating**
- Greet each person by name at the door. Hand them their name card and point them to their station.
- Collect any outstanding consent forms. Check the allergy line on every single one before anyone touches a product. Put a small coloured dot on the station of anyone with a flag.
- Water and refreshments available; students settle.

**0:05–0:10 — Welcome and the tone-setting speech**
- Who you are, in 60 seconds — not your CV, your reason. *"I've been doing makeup professionally for X years and the thing I hear most is 'I don't know where to start'. That's the entire reason this class exists."*
- The three promises: *"By the time you leave, you will have done a full face yourself, you will know why each step happens, and you'll have written notes so you don't have to remember any of it."*
- The three rules: **nobody comments on anybody else's face; there are no silly questions; if anything stings, stop and tell me.**
- Housekeeping: bathrooms, exits, break at the 90-minute mark, phones on silent but photos welcome at the end.
- Consent line, said out loud: *"I'll never touch your face without asking first, and 'no thanks' is a complete answer."*

---

### BLOCK 1 — Skin prep · 0:10–0:35

**0:10–0:15 — Why prep decides everything (talk)**
- The one-line thesis: *"Makeup doesn't sit on skin. It sits on whatever you put on your skin first. 80% of 'my foundation looks bad' is a prep problem, not a foundation problem."*
- The four skin behaviours, in plain language: oily (product slides), dry (product clings to flakes and creases), combination (both, in zones), sensitive (reacts). Not "skin types" as an identity — behaviours that change with season, stress and age.
- Quick self-diagnosis: *"Blot your forehead and cheek with a tissue. Where's the oil? That's your zone map for the next hour."*

**0:15–0:25 — Demo: cleanse → hydrate → prime**
- Cleanse: gentle, lukewarm, 30 seconds, pat dry — never rub.
- Hydrate: moisturiser matched to behaviour. Demonstrate the amount — a pea, not a dollop. **Emphasise the wait:** 2–3 minutes before anything goes on top. *"This wait is the most valuable thing I'll tell you all day. Every patchy foundation I've ever fixed was applied onto wet moisturiser."*
- SPF: non-negotiable, applied as the last skincare step. Two fingers' length for the face and neck.
- Primer: explain honestly — *"primer is optional and it is not magic. It's a targeted tool. Grip primer if your makeup slides, blurring primer for texture and pores, hydrating primer if your base looks patchy and dry."*
- Eye area and lips: eye cream, and lip balm on now so it has 20 minutes to work before you get to lips.

**0:25–0:35 — Students practise prep**
- Everyone cleanses, moisturises, applies SPF, applies lip balm.
- You circulate. Correct pressure (too hard, always) and quantity (too much, always).
- Use the wait time productively — this is your window to talk through the class kit list and answer skincare questions while their moisturiser absorbs. Do not let the room go silent here.

---

### BLOCK 2 — The natural everyday base · 0:35–1:10

**0:35–0:42 — Demo: colour matching and coverage strategy**
- Match on the **jawline, in natural light**. Never the wrist, never the back of the hand.
- Undertone in ten seconds: does gold or silver jewellery look better on you? Do you burn or tan? Warm, cool, neutral — and why a technically "correct" shade in the wrong undertone still looks like a mask.
- The core philosophy, said clearly: **"Foundation is not a coat of paint. It is a sheer wash that evens out, and then we spot-conceal the two or three things that actually bother us."**
- Demonstrate the difference live on half your face: full-coverage everywhere vs sheer base plus targeted concealer. This side-by-side is the moment the class "clicks" for most people.

**0:42–0:52 — Demo: application**
- Tools compared honestly: fingers (warms product, best for sheer/dewy, least control), damp sponge (most natural, absorbs product, best for beginners), brush (most coverage and control, most likely to look heavy in inexperienced hands).
- Method: three small dots — forehead, each cheek. Push and bounce outward toward the hairline and jaw. **Blend down the jaw and onto the neck, and past the ears.** Show them a hard demarcation line and then fix it.
- Concealer: **after** base, not before. Apply in a small triangle under the eye (not a half-moon), tap with a warm finger or damp sponge, less than you think. Second, tiny amount on blemishes with a small brush, pressed not swept.
- Setting: powder **only where you crease or shine** — usually the T-zone and under the eyes. *"Powdering the whole face is the single fastest way to make a natural base look like a cake."*

**0:52–1:10 — Students practise base**
- Half the face first. Everyone stops and compares halves in the mirror. This always gets a reaction.
- Then complete the second half themselves.
- You circulate constantly. The three fixes you will make in every class: too much product; not blending down the neck; scrubbing instead of bouncing.
- Photograph mid-blend halves for content (with the release checked — this is your best organic Instagram material).

---

### BLOCK 3 — Brows and soft eyes · 1:10–1:35

**1:10–1:16 — Demo: brows in 90 seconds**
- The three landmark points: start (in line with the inner corner), arch (roughly two-thirds out, above the outer iris), tail (a diagonal from the nose past the outer corner).
- The rule that saves everyone: **brush the hair up first, and fill only the gaps.** Light hairlike strokes in the direction of growth, then brush through to soften.
- Shade: one shade lighter than your hair for dark hair, one shade darker for very fair hair. Cool taupes over warm reds for most people.
- Set with a clear or tinted gel. Done. Do not carve, do not outline, do not use a shade of concealer around the brow.

**1:16–1:35 — Soft eye: demo and practise together, step by step**
Teach this in three commands and pause after each so everyone catches up.

1. **Transition shade** — a soft mid-tone, one to two shades deeper than the skin, on a fluffy brush, swept in the crease in windscreen-wiper motions. *"This is 70% of the look. If you only ever learn one eye step, it's this one."* For hooded eyes: place it slightly above the visible crease so it shows with the eye open.
2. **Depth in the outer corner** — a slightly deeper shade, small brush, outer third only, blended inward and upward. Small circles, never a straight line.
3. **Lift** — a light shimmer or satin on the inner corner and centre of the lid, patted on with a finger.

Then:
- **Liner (optional, taught as tightlining):** press a soft pencil into the base of the upper lashes rather than drawing a line on top. Gives definition with zero skill requirement and no risk of a wonky wing. Mention wings exist; explicitly say they are a Class Two skill and nobody should feel behind for not doing one today.
- **Mascara:** curl first (10 seconds, three positions along the lash). Wiggle at the base, then pull through. Upper lashes fully; lower lashes lightly or skip. **Single-use wand, into the bin.**
- Do a live fix of one "oops" — a mascara smudge on the lid — so everyone sees that mistakes are routine and repairable with a cotton bud, not a reason to start over.

---

### BLOCK 4 — Cheeks, lips and setting · 1:35–1:50

**1:35–1:42 — Demo: the ten-minute glow**
- **Blush placement by intent:** on the apples = youthful and round; slightly higher toward the temple = lifted; a soft sweep across the nose bridge = the current soft, sunburnt look. Show all three quickly on yourself, one cheek at a time.
- Cream before powder, always, if using both. Cream blush on bare-ish skin looks like skin; powder over cream lasts longer.
- **Bronzer, honestly explained:** warmth, not contour. A soft "3" from the temple, into the hollow of the cheek, along the jaw. Beginners should stop there — sculpting contour is a Class Two topic and this is exactly the step that makes a natural face look muddy.
- **Highlighter:** the top of the cheekbone, the bridge of the nose, the cupid's bow, the inner corner. Four small places, a light hand.
- **Lips:** balm is already on and has been absorbing for an hour. Blot, then a liner in a shade close to the natural lip to define and stop bleeding, then a satin or gloss nude/rose. Show the "over-lining" question honestly and neutrally: it exists, here is a subtle 1mm version, more than that is a look rather than an everyday face.

**1:42–1:50 — Students practise cheeks and lips, then setting**
- Setting spray: hold 20–30cm away, X and T pattern, do not soak. Press with clean hands to meld.
- Full-face reveal. Everyone looks up at once. This is the emotional high point of the class — let it breathe, take the group photo here.

---

### ☕ BREAK · 1:50–2:05 (15 minutes)

- Refreshments, bathroom, photos, informal questions.
- **This break is a business asset, not dead time.** Take individual photos of finished faces (release-checked). Have your rebooking card, private-lesson price list and kit list visible on the table. Ask two people directly: *"Would you like me to send you the notes and the product list tonight?"* — that's your email list.
- Answer the personal questions that people will not ask in front of the group. This is where private bookings come from.

---

### BLOCK 5 — The evening variation · 2:05–2:35

**2:05–2:12 — Demo: same face, five additions, thirty minutes**
The principle, stated up front: *"You do not remove your day makeup to go out. You add five things to it. That's the whole trick, and it's why you can do this in a car park."*

1. **Deepen the outer corner** — take the same deep shade further out and slightly up, and smudge a touch along the outer lower lash line.
2. **Add shimmer** to the centre of the lid, pressed with a finger. Pressing, never sweeping — sweeping just moves it around.
3. **Define the lash line** — tightline more heavily, or add a soft smudged line and a second coat of mascara. Strip or cluster lashes introduced here as optional, with the honest note that they take practice.
4. **Sharpen the face** — a touch more bronzer in the hollow, a stronger highlight on the cheekbone.
5. **A stronger lip** — one shade deeper than daytime, lined first, blotted, reapplied.

**2:12–2:32 — Students practise the evening look**
- Fast, energetic block. Music up slightly. Let people talk to each other.
- Circulate for the two failure points: shimmer applied with a sweeping motion, and outer-corner depth pulled too far down toward the cheek instead of up toward the tail of the brow.

**2:32–2:35 — Second reveal and photos**
- Day-to-night side-by-side photos on their own phones. Ask them to tag you. Give them the handle written on the take-home notes so nobody has to remember it.

---

### BLOCK 6 — Removal and skin recovery · 2:35–2:50

**2:35–2:42 — Demo: proper removal**
This block will earn you more repeat business than any other, because nobody teaches it.

- **Eyes first, and gently.** A cotton pad with micellar water or a bi-phase remover, **held still on the closed eye for 20 seconds** to dissolve, then wiped downward and outward once. *"Every time you scrub at your eyes you're pulling at the thinnest skin on your body and at your lashes. Twenty seconds of patience replaces thirty seconds of rubbing."*
- **Double cleanse:** an oil or balm cleanser massaged onto a **dry** face for 60 seconds to break down makeup and SPF, then emulsify with water, rinse. Follow with a gentle second cleanser.
- **The SPF point:** sunscreen is designed to be water-resistant. A single water-based cleanse does not remove it. This is why people who "always wash their face" still break out.
- **Never sleep in makeup.** If genuinely unable to face the sink: a cleansing wipe is better than nothing, and a proper cleanse in the morning. Wipes are a fallback, not a method.
- **After:** a hydrating toner or essence, moisturiser, and lip balm. Lashes need nothing.
- Practical notes: clean pillowcases twice a week; wash brushes weekly; the sponge is the dirtiest object in the kit.

**2:42–2:50 — Everyone removes their makeup together**
- Guided, unhurried, everyone doing it at the same time.
- Everyone leaves with clean, calm, moisturised skin. Nobody walks to their car feeling self-conscious about a full evening face at 4pm on a Saturday.

---

### BLOCK 7 — Close, notes and next steps · 2:50–3:00

**2:50–2:56 — Recap and take-home notes**
- Hand out the printed take-home notes (section 2.4). Walk the page for 60 seconds so they know what is on it.
- The recap, in one sentence each: prep decides everything; sheer base plus targeted concealer; brows fill the gaps; the transition shade is 70% of the eye; blush placement is intent; evening is five additions; removal is twenty seconds of patience.
- Ask the room: *"What's the one thing you're going to do differently tomorrow morning?"* Go around. It cements the learning, and it is the best testimonial material you will ever collect — write the answers down.

**2:56–3:00 — Offers and goodbye**
- Feedback card or QR code on every seat (three questions, 20 seconds).
- Name the next step clearly and once, without pressure: *"If you want to go further — Class Two covers wings, lashes, contour and colour. If you'd rather work on your own face specifically, a private session does exactly that. Details are on the back of your notes, and there's a returning-student rate."*
- Group photo. Thank each person by name at the door.

## 2.3 What students walk away knowing

**They can do, unassisted:**
- Identify how their skin is behaving today and prep it accordingly
- Colour-match a base on the jawline in natural light
- Apply a sheer, even base with a sponge and spot-conceal only what needs it
- Set strategically instead of powdering everything
- Fill brows using the three landmark points and hairlike strokes
- Build a three-step soft eye: transition, outer depth, inner lift
- Tightline and apply mascara safely, from a curled lash
- Place blush, bronzer and highlighter with intent rather than habit
- Line and fill a lip so it lasts
- Convert a day face into an evening face in under ten minutes using five additions
- Remove makeup and SPF properly, without dragging the eye area

**They understand:**
- Why prep, waiting time, and product quantity matter more than product price
- That coverage is a choice, not a default
- Their own face shape, eye shape and undertone, in practical terms
- Basic hygiene: double-dipping, single-use wands, sharpening, brush washing, expiry dates
- Which products are worth spending on and which are not
- That mistakes are fixable with a cotton bud, and that starting over is almost never necessary

**They leave with:** a finished face they did themselves, printed notes, a shopping list, a day-to-night photo, and a clear picture of what to practise next.

**Makeup by Aphrodite — THE EVERYDAY MASTERCLASS**
*Everything we covered today, on one page.*

**THE ORDER OF OPERATIONS**
Cleanse → Moisturise → **wait 2–3 min** → SPF → Primer (optional) → Base → Concealer → Powder (T-zone only) → Brows → Eyes → Blush → Bronzer → Highlight → Lips → Setting spray

**SKIN PREP**
- Cleanse gently, lukewarm water, pat dry — never rub.
- Moisturiser: a pea for the whole face. Then wait. This wait is the difference between smooth and patchy.
- SPF every single day, indoors and in winter. Two fingers' length for face and neck.
- Primer is a targeted tool, not magic: grip (if it slides), blurring (texture), hydrating (patchiness).

**BASE**
- Match on the **jawline**, in **natural light**.
- Three dots — forehead, both cheeks. Bounce outward. Blend down the neck and past the ears.
- Sheer base + targeted concealer beats full coverage everywhere.
- Concealer **after** base. Small triangle under the eye, tapped in. Less than you think.
- Powder only where you shine or crease.

**BROWS — 90 seconds**
Brush hair **up** first → fill only the gaps with light hairlike strokes → brush through → set with gel.
Landmarks: start above the inner corner · arch two-thirds out · tail on a diagonal from the nose past the outer corner.

**EYES — three steps, in order**
1. **Transition:** mid-tone in the crease, fluffy brush, windscreen-wiper motion. *This is 70% of the look.*
2. **Depth:** deeper shade, outer third only, blended in and **up**.
3. **Lift:** shimmer or satin on the inner corner and lid centre — **pressed with a finger, never swept**.
Then: tightline (press pencil into the lash base) · curl lashes 10 seconds · mascara wiggle at the base, pull through.

**CHEEKS**
- Blush is placement, not colour: apples = youthful · higher toward the temple = lifted · across the nose = soft/sunkissed.
- Cream before powder, always.
- Bronzer is **warmth, not contour** — a soft "3" from temple to jaw.
- Highlight in four places: cheekbone, nose bridge, cupid's bow, inner corner.

**LIPS**
Balm early → blot → line close to your natural shape → fill → satin or gloss on top.

**DAY → EVENING IN 10 MINUTES — the five additions**
1. Deepen the outer corner, blend **up**
2. Press shimmer on the lid centre
3. Define the lash line + second coat of mascara
4. More bronzer in the hollow, stronger highlight
5. A deeper lip — line, blot, reapply

**REMOVAL — the part nobody teaches**
- Eyes first: hold a soaked pad still for **20 seconds**, then wipe down and out. Once. No scrubbing.
- **Double cleanse:** oil or balm on a **dry** face for 60 seconds → emulsify → rinse → gentle second cleanse.
- A water-based cleanse alone does **not** remove SPF. This is the breakout culprit.
- Then: toner/essence → moisturiser → lip balm.
- Never sleep in makeup. Wipes are a fallback, not a method.

**HYGIENE — the professional habits**
Never double-dip · single-use mascara wands · sharpen pencils before use · spot-clean brushes daily and deep-clean weekly · dry your sponge fully and replace it monthly · pillowcase twice a week
**Expiry:** mascara & liquid liner 3 months · liquid foundation 12 months · powders 24 months

**MY FOUR-WEEK PRACTICE PLAN**
- Week 1: prep and base only. No eyes. Get the base flawless.
- Week 2: add brows and the transition shade.
- Week 3: add the full three-step eye.
- Week 4: full face in under 15 minutes, timed.
