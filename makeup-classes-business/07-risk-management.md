# 7. Risk Management — Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them

---

## 7.1 Mixed-age teaching: the ten failure modes

### 1. Pitching at the adults and losing the teens
**What it looks like:** you use words like "occlusive", "demarcation", "colour theory", talk about wedding makeup, and reference products teens have never heard of. Two 15-year-olds go quiet, then get on their phones.
**Avoid it:** pitch every explanation at the youngest person in the room. Define any term the first time you use it, in five words. Adults are never annoyed by clarity — they're relieved by it. Use examples that span ages: "school formal or work Christmas party."

### 2. Teens finish fast, adults need time — and the room splits
**What it looks like:** teens are done with the base in six minutes and bored; adults are still blending at fourteen and feel rushed and embarrassed.
**Avoid it:** have a challenge ready for fast finishers every single block — *"now do it again with your non-dominant hand"*, *"now do it in 60 seconds"*, *"now try it with a brush instead of a sponge and tell me which you prefer"*. Never let a fast finisher sit and watch a slow one. And never say "we're waiting on a few people" — say "take the time you need, I've got a bonus for anyone who's finished."

### 3. A teen gets self-conscious being watched
**What it looks like:** you use a 14-year-old as a demo model or point out their work to the room. They freeze, go red, and disengage for the rest of the class.
**Avoid it:** demonstrate on yourself, always. Never single out a minor, even to praise them publicly — praise them quietly, at their station, where nobody else hears. If you want a demo model, use an adult who volunteers, or a paid model.

### 4. An adult comments on a teenager's skin
**What it looks like:** a well-meaning 50-year-old says *"oh you don't need any of this at your age, you've got beautiful skin"* or worse, *"a bit of concealer will sort that out"*. The teen's afternoon is ruined.
**Avoid it:** the "nobody comments on anybody else's face" rule is stated in the first two minutes of every class, warmly and as a shared value, not a warning. *"We've got a mix of ages today, which is my favourite kind of room. One rule: we don't comment on each other's faces — not even compliments about what someone 'doesn't need'. Just your own mirror."* If it happens anyway, redirect immediately and lightly: *"And that's exactly why we do our own faces here! [Name], how are you finding that transition shade?"*

### 5. Teaching age-inappropriate content
**What it looks like:** heavy contour, over-lining, full-coverage base, strip lashes and a smoky eye taught to 14-year-olds — which parents notice, dislike, and mention to other parents.
**Avoid it:** run the teen curriculum for teens (skin prep heavy, minimal coverage, soft brown eye, tinted lip). In genuinely mixed rooms, offer the evening block as a "formal look" variation for the younger students — same techniques, lower intensity. Never say the words "problem areas", "fix", or "cover that up" to anyone, but especially not to a teenager.

### 6. A parent hovers, corrects, or takes over
**What it looks like:** a parent stands behind their daughter, criticises her blending, and does it for her. The teen shuts down completely.
**Avoid it:** decide the parent's role *before* the class starts, at the door, warmly: *"You're so welcome to stay — there's tea and a comfy seat at the back. I'll ask you to let her make her own mistakes though, that's genuinely how this works best."* Give hovering parents something to do: hold the camera, be the timekeeper. In Mother & Daughter classes, give the parent their own station and their own face to work on. It solves the problem entirely.

### 7. The unspoken competition
**What it looks like:** students glance sideways, compare, and the least confident person in the room stops trying.
**Avoid it:** angle stations so mirrors face students, not each other. Say out loud, early: *"You will all end up with different faces today, and that's the entire point — I'm teaching you your face, not mine."* Never rank, never compare, never say "look how well [name] has done that."

### 8. Safeguarding assumptions
**What it looks like:** you snap a lovely group photo and post it. One student's parent ticked "no photography" three weeks ago and you'd forgotten.
**Avoid it:** photo status goes on the name card at every station — a small coloured dot system (green = full, amber = makeup close-ups only, red = none). Check the cards, not your memory. Before any group shot, say: *"Group photo — anyone who'd rather not be in it, just step to the side, no explanation needed."*

### 9. Talking about bodies, weight or "fixing" features
**What it looks like:** a throwaway line about slimming the nose, snatching the jaw, or looking tired. In a room with a teenager, that lands very differently than it does among adult friends.
**Avoid it:** language discipline. Bronzer "warms". Highlighter "catches the light". Blush "adds life". Nothing is slimmed, snatched, fixed or corrected. If a student asks how to make their nose look smaller, answer the technique neutrally and briefly, then redirect: *"That's shading — here's how it works. Honestly though, the highest-value thing on your face is what we did with the base."*

### 10. Being alone with a minor
**What it looks like:** everyone leaves, one student's lift is late, and you're alone in a closed room with a 15-year-old for 25 minutes.
**Avoid it:** stay in a visible, open space. Wait outside or in the foyer with them. Text the parent immediately: *"All finished — [Student] is with me in the foyer, no rush."* This protects the student and it protects you, and the text creates a record.

## 7.2 Operational pitfalls

| Pitfall | What happens | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| **Running out of shade range** | Someone with deep skin, or very fair skin with pink undertones, can't be matched. It is humiliating for them and fatal for your reputation. | 12+ foundation shades before class one, no exceptions. This is the one place not to economise. |
| **Overrunning the clock** | You spend 50 minutes on base, then rush eyes, skip removal, and finish 25 minutes late. | Do the timed practice run. Put a silent timer where only you can see it. Have a "cut list" of what you'll drop — cut the bronzer demo, never the removal block. |
| **Bad lighting** | Colour matches are wrong, faces look grey, your photos are unusable, and students say the class "looked better in the room". | Two 5500K daylight panels. Visit the venue at your actual class hour. Do every colour match at the window. |
| **Under-filled classes** | Three students at $115 loses money after venue and consumables. | Minimum of 4 to run. Be willing to postpone at 7 days out — announce it as "moving to a better date", carry deposits over, and offer a small credit. |
| **No-shows** | Two empty seats, product prepped, revenue gone. | Non-refundable $50 deposit + auto-balance at 48 hours + a 24-hour SMS. This combination virtually eliminates no-shows. |
| **Forgotten consent forms** | A minor is in your room without signed consent. | Consent form is a mandatory field in the online booking flow. Ten printed spares at every class. **A student without a form does not touch a product** — sit them with you and complete it, or call the parent. |
| **A reaction mid-class** | A student's eye reddens and stings. | Stop, remove with water and a clean pad, cool compress, sit them down, offer water. Do not apply anything else. Record product and batch. Advise pharmacist/GP. Log it same day. Follow up next day. |
| **Cold sore or eye infection presenting** | Contaminates your entire kit. | Visual check as people arrive. If you see an active cold sore or conjunctivitis, take them aside privately, be kind and matter-of-fact, and move them to the next class free. *"I'd love to have you but I can't work near an active cold sore — it's a kit hygiene thing, not you. Let me move you to [date] at no cost."* |
| **Kit contamination** | Double-dipping, a returned mascara wand. | Decant everything before class. Wands in one jar, bin beside it. Say the rule out loud three times during the class — it's content, not nagging. |
| **Photos posted without release** | A furious parent, a defensible complaint, potentially an insurance issue. | The coloured-dot system on name cards. Sort photos by release status the same day and **delete non-release faces immediately** — don't "keep them just in case". |
| **Teaching outside your scope** | You give skincare or acne advice, someone's skin worsens, they blame you. | One sentence, used every time: *"That's outside what I can advise on — a pharmacist or GP is the right person, and they see it constantly."* You teach application, not dermatology. |
| **Insurance gap** | Your policy covers artistry on clients but not instruction. You discover this after an incident. | Call your insurer this week and confirm in writing that **teaching and instruction to members of the public, including minors,** is covered. This is the single most common uninsured gap in this business. |
| **Being underpriced and overbooked** | You run four classes a month at $115, exhaust yourself, and earn less than you did doing bridal. | Raise prices when you sell out two classes in a row. Push privates and Class Two — better margin, less setup, less physical toll. |
| **Burnout** | Setup, three hours on your feet, pack-down, brush washing, then content editing at 10pm. | Cap at four classes a month in the first six months. Wash brushes at the venue, not at midnight. Batch content one afternoon a fortnight. Store the trolley on site if you can. |
| **Trusting your memory** | Allergy flags, photo status, who wants the private lesson. | Name cards with dots. A clipboard. The incident log. Write it down, always. |

## 7.3 Reputational risk

- **Never post a "before" that reads as a criticism.** Frame it as "bare skin" and "after class", or use half-face progress shots. Nobody's face was a problem to be solved.
- **Never post a student's face with your venue location and their handle in the same post** — particularly a minor's.
- **Respond to negative feedback once, publicly, warmly, without defending yourself**, then move it to private. *"Thank you for telling me — I'm sorry it wasn't what you hoped for. I've sent you a message so I can make it right."*
- **Don't overpromise in your marketing.** "You'll leave knowing how to do your own everyday makeup" is true and deliverable. "You'll be a makeup artist" is not, and the gap between promise and delivery is where refund requests come from.
- **Be honest about products.** If you recommend the $18 option over the $80 one, people trust everything else you say. This is your biggest long-term differentiator against brand-sponsored educators.
- **Never disparage another local artist or class.** Small towns talk, and it always reaches them.

## 7.4 The one-page pre-class safety check

Laminate this. Run it before every single class, without exception.

**48 hours before**
- [ ] Consent forms received for every attendee — check each one individually
- [ ] Allergy and medical flags read and noted on name cards
- [ ] Photo release status marked with dots on name cards
- [ ] Under-18s: parent contact number confirmed and saved in your phone
- [ ] Second adult confirmed if any minor is attending without a parent staying
- [ ] Balances charged

**Morning of**
- [ ] All products expiry-checked
- [ ] All brushes clean and dry, bagged per student
- [ ] Creams and liquids decanted; spatulas ready
- [ ] Powders sanitised with isopropyl
- [ ] Pencils sharpened
- [ ] Disposables stocked: wands, sponges, applicators, cotton, tissues
- [ ] First aid kit and saline in the trolley
- [ ] Sanitiser at every station
- [ ] Bin with liner and lid
- [ ] Phone charged, on, and audible

**On arrival at the venue**
- [ ] Surfaces wiped down
- [ ] Emergency exits and first aid location identified
- [ ] Lighting set and tested on a real face
- [ ] Stations laid out with name cards and dots visible
- [ ] Spare consent forms on the table
- [ ] Incident log in the trolley

**First two minutes of class**
- [ ] Three rules stated: no comments on faces · no silly questions · stinging means stop
- [ ] Consent line said out loud: *"I'll never touch your face without asking, and 'no thanks' is a complete answer."*
- [ ] Photo policy stated: *"I'll be taking a few photos — if you'd rather not be in them, tell me now or any time later, no explanation needed."*
- [ ] Exits and bathrooms pointed out
- [ ] Break time announced
