# 6. Financial Starter Guidance & Lean Launch

> Figures in AUD, based on a solo operator with existing kit, an existing venue arrangement, and existing insurance. Adjust to your actual venue hire and product costs. This is business planning guidance, not accounting or tax advice — see a bookkeeper once before your first BAS.

## 6.3 The first six months — a realistic model

Assumes you run **2 classes a month** in months 1–2, **3 a month** in months 3–4, **4 a month** in months 5–6, with fill rates that improve as word of mouth builds. Private sessions grow from zero as group students convert.

| | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 | M6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Group classes run | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 |
| Avg students per class | 5 | 6 | 7 | 7 | 8 | 8 |
| Price per student | $115* | $115* | $145 | $145 | $145 | $145 |
| **Group revenue** | $1,150 | $1,380 | $3,045 | $3,045 | $4,640 | $4,640 |
| Private sessions | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| **Private revenue** | $0 | $260 | $520 | $780 | $1,040 | $1,300 |
| Parties / duos | 0 | 0 | 1 ($450) | 1 ($450) | 2 ($900) | 2 ($900) |
| Starter kit add-ons | $150 | $225 | $375 | $375 | $600 | $600 |
| **TOTAL REVENUE** | **$1,300** | **$1,865** | **$4,390** | **$4,650** | **$7,180** | **$7,440** |
| Variable costs | $620 | $680 | $1,150 | $1,180 | $1,650 | $1,680 |
| Fixed costs | $120 | $120 | $120 | $150 | $150 | $150 |
| Marketing spend | $150 | $150 | $200 | $200 | $250 | $250 |
| **TOTAL COSTS** | **$890** | **$950** | **$1,470** | **$1,530** | **$2,050** | **$2,080** |
| **NET** | **$410** | **$915** | **$2,920** | **$3,120** | **$5,130** | **$5,360** |
| **Cumulative net** | $410 | $1,325 | $4,245 | $7,365 | $12,495 | **$17,855** |

*\* Founding price for the first three classes only.*

**Break-even on startup costs: around the end of month 3.** Cumulative net crosses the ~$2,500 startup figure partway through month 3.

**Be honest with yourself about the assumptions.** The two that most often fail:
1. **Fill rate.** A five-person class at $115 is *fine*. A three-person class is a loss after venue and consumables. Set a minimum of 4 and be willing to postpone — a postponed class costs you goodwill; a half-empty one costs you money and momentum.
2. **Private conversion.** The model assumes roughly 1 in 6 group students eventually books a private or Class Two. That only happens if you mention it, once, warmly, at the close of every class, and follow up on day 12. If you skip that, halve the private line.

## 6.4 Pricing guidance for months 1–6

**Months 1–2 — Prove it and photograph it.**
Run at the $115 founding price, advertised explicitly as *"founding price for my first three classes"*. The goal of these classes is not profit — it is 15 photographs, 8 testimonials, and the muscle memory of running a room. Accept a class of 5.

**Month 3 — Go to full price and don't apologise.**
$145. Announce it plainly: *"The founding price was for my first three classes and those have now run. Regular price from here is $145."* Nobody objects when the reason is stated. If you have testimonials and results photos by now — and you will — the price is easy to hold.

**Months 4–6 — Widen, don't discount.**
When bookings soften, the instinct is to cut price. Don't. Add products instead:
- Mother & Daughter classes (school holidays)
- Teen classes (school holidays, sell out fastest)
- Birthday parties (highest per-hour revenue, almost no marketing needed)
- Corporate workshops (one email to three local firms is worth trying)
- Gift vouchers (push November and April)
- Class Two (retention — this is where your margin lives long-term)

**The rule:** every discount must have a name and a reason — founding price, bring-a-friend, returning student, gift voucher, off-peak weekday. Never an unexplained price drop.

**When to raise prices again:** when you sell out two consecutive classes within a week of announcing them. Then go to $165. Repeat.

## 6.5 Money admin — set this up in week one

- [ ] **Get an ABN** (free, online, 15 minutes).
- [ ] **Register your business name** if trading as "Makeup by Aphrodite" and it isn't your legal name (~$45 for 3 years).
- [ ] **Open a separate bank account** for the business. Non-negotiable. Everything in and out goes through it.
- [ ] **Set aside 25–30% of every payment** in a second account for tax. Do it the day the money lands, not at tax time.
- [ ] **GST:** you must register once turnover hits $75,000 in a 12-month period. On the model above you're well under that in year one — but track it monthly so it doesn't surprise you.
- [ ] **Keep every receipt.** Photograph it into Wave/Xero the same day. Your kit, venue hire, insurance, printing, phone portion, travel and Canva subscription are all deductible business expenses. Your kit purchases are the biggest deduction you have.
- [ ] **Log mileage** if driving to the venue.
- [ ] **Reconcile weekly**, 20 minutes on a Sunday. Monthly reconciliation always turns into a four-hour nightmare.
- [ ] **See a bookkeeper once**, before your first BAS or tax return. One hour of their time saves several of yours and usually pays for itself.

## 6.6 Lean launch checklist

### Phase 1 — Foundations (week 1)
- [ ] ABN registered
- [ ] Business name registered
- [ ] Separate business bank account opened
- [ ] Working With Children Check applied for (do this first — processing takes time)
- [ ] Insurance certificate located; confirm it covers **teaching/instruction**, not just artistry on clients. Call them and ask explicitly — this is a real coverage gap and it takes one phone call to close.
- [ ] Venue booked for first three class dates
- [ ] Venue walk-through completed at your actual class time (see §3.5 checklist)
- [ ] Consent form reviewed by insurer or solicitor
- [ ] First aid course booked

### Phase 2 — Systems (week 2)
- [ ] Square Appointments (or Fresha) set up with the three class dates
- [ ] Consent + medical form built in Jotform, wired as a required step before payment
- [ ] $50 deposit + auto-balance-at-48-hrs configured
- [ ] Automated confirmation, 24-hr reminder and thank-you messages loaded (templates in §5)
- [ ] MailerLite account with the day-3 and day-12 follow-ups queued
- [ ] Google Business Profile created (free, and it drives local search — do not skip)
- [ ] Google review link generated and saved
- [ ] Cancellation policy published on the booking page
- [ ] Incident log created

### Phase 3 — Brand and materials (week 2–3)
- [ ] Instagram business account created, bio set, name field optimised for local search
- [ ] Facebook page created (older demographic and community groups still live here)
- [ ] Logo — a wordmark in a light serif is completely sufficient to start
- [ ] Brand colours and two fonts locked into Canva as a brand kit
- [ ] 8 promotional images generated (see §4.7)
- [ ] Flyer designed — A4 and A5
- [ ] Take-home notes designed and 100 printed
- [ ] Feedback cards printed with a QR to the Google review link
- [ ] Business cards printed (250 is plenty)
- [ ] 6 highlight covers made

### Phase 4 — Kit and room (week 3)
- [ ] Foundation shade range audited — at least 12 shades across warm, cool and neutral
- [ ] 10 brush sets purchased, washed, dried, bagged individually
- [ ] Disposables bulk-purchased
- [ ] Every product expiry-checked and sticker-dated
- [ ] Daylight lights purchased and tested in the actual room
- [ ] Trolley packed with a written pack list taped inside the lid
- [ ] Full dry run: set the room up alone, timed. If setup takes more than 45 minutes, simplify.
- [ ] Practice run of the full 3-hour class on two friends, free, with a timer. **Do not skip this.** You will discover the plan runs 20 minutes long, and you'll find out where — this is what the practice run is for.

### Phase 5 — Launch (week 4)
- [ ] Two weeks of content pre-made and scheduled
- [ ] Launch Reel filmed and edited
- [ ] Announcement post live
- [ ] Flyers up: community centre, library, cafés, gyms, hairdressers, high schools (with permission), community noticeboards
- [ ] Posted in 3–5 local Facebook community groups (read their promo rules first)
- [ ] Personally messaged 20 people who'd genuinely want this — this fills your first class faster than every other channel combined
- [ ] Told your existing makeup clients — your warmest audience by a distance
- [ ] Asked the community centre to include it in their newsletter or noticeboard
- [ ] Waitlist set up for when a class fills

### Phase 6 — After class one (same week)
- [ ] Every consent form filed securely
- [ ] Photos sorted by release status, non-release faces deleted immediately
- [ ] Thank-you messages sent within 4 hours
- [ ] Feedback cards read; one thing identified to change for next time
- [ ] Results carousel posted
- [ ] Review requests sent at day 5
- [ ] Kit restocked and brushes washed **before** you leave the venue
- [ ] Incident log updated (even if the entry is "nil")
- [ ] Actual costs recorded against the estimates above, and the model updated with your real numbers

## 6.7 The three numbers to watch monthly

1. **Fill rate** (students ÷ seats). Below 60% means your marketing or your dates are wrong, not your price.
2. **Repeat/referral rate** (students who rebook or send someone). Above 20% means the class itself is genuinely good and you should be scaling dates, not spending on ads.
3. **Revenue per teaching hour** (total revenue ÷ hours in the room, including setup). Should climb past $200/hr by month 4. If it isn't, you're underpricing or under-filling — and privates are the fastest fix for both.
